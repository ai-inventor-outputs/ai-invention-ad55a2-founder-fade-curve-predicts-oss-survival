> **TL;DR** — We rigorously tested the scaffolding-fade hypothesis — that gradual founder involvement reduction predicts OSS project survival — on 309 real projects from the ESEM 2019 dataset. The hypothesis is disconfirmed: fade descriptors perform below chance (AUC=0.462), add no value beyond static features, and show reversed directionality. Post-departure activity, not pre-departure founder trajectories, drives survival prediction. This negative result rules out a theoretically motivated mechanism and sharpens the question of what actually enables OSS projects to survive their founders.

<details>
<summary>Full hypothesis</summary>

The shape of a founder's involvement trajectory (fade curve) does not predict open-source project survival after departure, and provides no complementary predictive value beyond static project metrics. Despite the theoretical plausibility of importing educational scaffolding-with-fading theory into OSS sustainability, empirical testing on the ESEM2019 dataset (309 projects) shows fade-only models perform below chance (AUC=0.462), the directional effect reverses (collapsed projects have higher fade indices), and a falsification control confirms no genuine signal. The dominant predictor of survival is post-departure activity, not pre-departure founder trajectories, suggesting that OSS survival is determined by what happens after the founder leaves rather than how the founder departed. This null result rules out one theoretically motivated mechanism and sharpens the question of what actually enables OSS projects to survive their founders.

</details>
